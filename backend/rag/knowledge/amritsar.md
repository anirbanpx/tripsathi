# Amritsar Travel Knowledge

## Routing

**Standard routing: Delhi → Amritsar → Chandigarh**

Starting from Delhi, the national highway (NH 44) brings you straight to Amritsar without major climbs, allowing a relaxed first leg. Amritsar sits at the north‑west tip of Punjab; moving south‑east to Chandigarh avoids back‑tracking and lets you finish the loop on a flatter stretch of NH 5. This order minimizes fatigue, keeps fuel stops predictable, and aligns with typical rail‑to‑road itineraries for families and senior travellers.

Driving times:  
- Delhi → Amritsar: 8 hours (NH 44, Delhi‑Amritsar Expressway)  
- Amritsar → Chandigarh: 4.5 hours (NH 5 via Jalandhar, well‑maintained four‑lane)

**Alternative routing:** Enter Punjab from the west via the Wagah Border (India–Pakistan crossing) and proceed eastward through Amritsar to Ludhiana, then on to Chandigarh. This circuit is favoured by solo backpackers who want a border‑view experience before heading inland.

## Amritsar City

**Key places:** Golden Temple, Jallianwala Bagh, Partition Museum, Durgiana Temple, Hall Bazaar, Gobindgarh Fort

**Golden Temple (Harmandir Sahib):**  
- Entry: Free (₹ 30 donation optional for maintenance)  
- Suitability: Flat marble platform, wheelchair‑friendly ramps; **suitable for toddlers (stroller‑accessible) and elderly travellers**. No steep steps, but the surrounding crowd can be dense during early morning prayers.  
- Best time of day: Early morning (5:30–7:00 am) for serene ambience and cooler temperatures.  
- Seasonal closure: None; open year‑round, though the kitchen (Langar) may pause during major festivals for cleaning.

**Jallianwala Bagh:**  
- Entry: Free (₹ 10 for a guided audio device)  
- Suitability: Open lawn with paved paths; **suitable for toddlers and elderly**, but the central monument involves a short climb of 10 m; assist those with mobility limits.  
- Best time of day: Late afternoon (4–6 pm) when the light softens for photography.  
- Seasonal closure: None; the site remains accessible even during monsoon, though puddles may form on peripheral walkways.

**Partition Museum:**  
- Entry: ₹ 150 adult, ₹ 75 child (3–12 yr) – toddlers under 3 free  
- Suitability: Indoor galleries with climate control; **ideal for families with young kids** (interactive displays) and seniors (seating benches). Not wheelchair‑friendly for the upper‑floor exhibit (requires stairs).  
- Best time of day: Mid‑morning (10–12 am) to avoid the post‑lunch crowd.  
- Seasonal closure: Closed on Mondays and during major religious festivals (e.g., Guru Nanak Jayanti).

**Trust issue or pricing trap for this area:**  
- **Unauthorized “photo‑shoot” guides** near the Golden Temple often charge ₹ 500–₹ 800 for a 10‑minute session, promising “instant passport‑style photos.”  
- Recommended approach: Use the official Langar Hall’s free photo booths or reputable studios in Hall Bazaar that display clear price lists; negotiate or walk away from any guide who does not show identification.

**Amritsar city food:**  
- Signature dishes: Amritsari kulcha, chole bhature, tandoori chicken, butter naan, lassi, jalebi.  
- For toddlers and young kids: Soft plain kulcha with butter, khichdi (rice‑lentil porridge), steamed idli, plain dal with rice, fresh mango slices.  
- Vegetarian availability: Very high; most street stalls and dhabas serve pure‑vegetarian versions of kulcha, chole, and paneer tikka. Meat dishes are clearly labelled, so veg travellers can avoid them easily.

## Surrounding Punjab (Tarn Taran & Anandpur Sahib)

**Key places:** Tarn Taran Sahib (Gurdwara), Takht Sri Keshgarh Sahib (Anandpur Sahib), Maharaja Ranjit Singh Museum, Rural craft villages

**Tarn Taran Sahib (Gurdwara):**  
- Entry: Free (₹ 20 optional donation)  
- Suitability: Large sarovar (holy pool) with gentle ramps; **suitable for toddlers (stroller‑friendly) and elderly**. The surrounding garden has uneven stones, so assist those with walking aids.  
- Best time of day: Early evening (6–7 pm) to witness the Palki Sahib ceremony.  
- Seasonal closure: None; the sarovar is maintained year‑round.

**Takht Sri Keshgarh Sahib (Anandpur Sahib):**  
- Entry: Free (₹ 50 for audio guide)  
- Suitability: Flat courtyard with paved walkways; **suitable for seniors and families with young kids**. The inner sanctum requires climbing a short flight of stairs (≈12 steps).  
- Best time of day: Morning (9–11 am) before the large pilgrim crowd gathers.  
- Seasonal closure: Closed on major Sikh holidays (e.g., Vaisakhi) for special prayers; entry resumes the next day.

**Maharaja Ranjit Singh Museum:**  
- Entry: ₹ 120 adult, ₹ 60 child (3–12 yr) – toddlers free  
- Suitability: Indoor, air‑conditioned; **ideal for seniors and kids**; wheelchair access is limited to the ground floor.  
- Best time of day: Mid‑afternoon (2–4 pm) when the museum is less crowded.  
- Seasonal closure: Closed on Tuesdays.

**Trust issue or pricing trap for this area:**  
- **Overcharging for “guided temple tours”** by unregistered drivers who quote ₹ 1,000 for a 30‑minute ride plus “guide fees.”  
- Recommended approach: Hire a licensed auto‑rickshaw (metered) and request a certified guide from the Gurdwara’s information desk; they charge a transparent ₹ 200‑₹ 300 per group.

**Surrounding Punjab food:**  
- Signature dishes: Makki di roti with sarson da saag, Punjabi kadhi, bhindi masala, lassi, jalebi.  
- For toddlers and young kids: Soft makki roti with a thin layer of ghee, plain boiled potatoes, plain rice with a spoonful of ghee, fresh curd.  
- Vegetarian availability: Predominantly vegetarian; most eateries serve both veg and non‑veg, with clear signage. Vegan options are limited but can be requested (e.g., dal without ghee).

## General Amritsar Notes

**Best time to visit:**  
- **October – March:** Cool (10‑20 °C), festivals (Diwali, Guru Nanak Jayanti) boost cultural activities; all attractions fully open.  
- **April – June:** Hot (30‑40 °C); early mornings are comfortable, but midday heat can be taxing for toddlers and seniors; indoor museums provide respite.  
- **July – September:** Monsoon (occasional heavy rain); Golden Temple’s marble can become slippery, so use anti‑slip footwear; some outdoor sites (Jallianwala Bagh) may have water‑logged paths, but most attractions remain accessible.

**Transport pricing:**  
- **Taxi/cab:** Metered fares start at ₹ 250 for the first 3 km, then ₹ 12 per km; negotiate only for out‑station trips, not city rides.  
- **Auto‑rickshaw:** Fixed‑rate for short hops (≈₹ 30‑₹ 50 for 2 km); always ask for the meter or confirm the fare before boarding.  
- **App‑based options:** Ola and Uber operate in Amritsar; UberX fares average ₹ 180 for a 5 km city ride. Use the app for transparent pricing and driver tracking.

**Dietary notes:**  
- Dominant cuisine: Rich Punjabi fare heavy on ghee, butter, and dairy; common allergens include gluten (wheat flour) and dairy (curd, paneer). Pork is rarely served, but some “Mughlai” restaurants may offer it—check menus if avoiding pork.  
- Vegetarian options: Abundant; most street stalls and dhabas label dishes as “veg” or “non‑veg.” Pure‑vegetarian eateries are common in Hall Bazaar and near the Golden Temple.

**Medical:**  
- Nearest quality hospital: **Guru Nanak Dev Hospital** (Government tertiary care, located on Circular Road, ~3 km from the Golden Temple). It offers 24‑hour emergency services, a well‑equipped ICU, and English‑speaking staff.  
- What to carry: Basic first‑aid kit, any personal medication, a copy of health insurance, and a list of allergies (especially dairy or gluten). For seniors, bring a portable blood pressure monitor; for toddlers, pack oral rehydration salts in case of diarrhoea.
