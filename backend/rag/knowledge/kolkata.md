# Kolkata Travel Knowledge

## Routing

**Standard routing: Howrah → Kolkata → Sundarbans (Godkhali entry point)**  

Why this order: Arriving via Howrah Bridge lets travellers acclimatise to the city’s traffic patterns before tackling Kolkata’s dense core. From Kolkata, the journey south to the Sundarbans follows the NH‑12 (Kolkata‑Malda Road), a well‑maintained highway with frequent fuel stops, reducing fatigue before the longer boat‑transfer to the mangroves.

Driving times:  
- Howrah → Kolkata: ≈ 0.5 hours (Vidyasagar Setu / Second Hooghly Bridge, NH‑2)  
- Kolkata → Sundarbans (Godkhali): ≈ 4.5 hours (NH‑12, then local roads to the ferry jetty)

**Alternative routing:** Enter from the north via Siliguri → Kolkata → Howrah → Sundarbans, useful for travellers coming by train from the Northeast and wanting to experience the city before heading south.

## North Kolkata

**Key places:** College Street, Kumartuli, Indian Museum, St. Paul’s Cathedral

**College Street Book Market:**  
- Entry: Free (optional ₹20 for a guided walk)  
- Suitability: Flat sidewalks, but narrow aisles; **not suitable for toddlers** under 3 due to crowding, **elderly** may find uneven cobbles tiring.  
- Best time: Early morning (7–10 am) to avoid heat and crowds.  
- Seasonal closure: None, but monsoon (July‑September) can make the area slippery.

**Kumartuli (Potters’ Quarter):**  
- Entry: Free (donations ₹50 adults, ₹30 children)  
- Suitability: Small lanes, **not toddler‑friendly**, **moderate for elderly** (some steps).  
- Best time: Late afternoon (4–6 pm) when artisans are active.  
- Seasonal closure: Closed on major Hindu festivals (e.g., Durga Puja) when workshops are private.

**Indian Museum:**  
- Entry: ₹25 adult, ₹10 child (3‑12 yr); **free for toddlers** under 3.  
- Suitability: Wheelchair‑ramp available; **elderly** can navigate comfortably, **toddlers** need supervision near artifact displays.  
- Best time: Mid‑day (12‑2 pm) to avoid school‑group rush.  
- Seasonal closure: Closed on Mondays and major national holidays.

**Trust issue for North Kolkata:**  
- Scam: “Book‑stall overcharging” – vendors often quote inflated prices for rare books to tourists.  
- Recommended approach: Compare prices online beforehand, ask locals for the average rate, and politely decline if the markup exceeds ₹200 per book.

**North Kolkata food:**  
- Signature dishes: Kathi Roll (egg or paneer), Macher Jhal (spicy fish curry), Mishti Doi (sweet yogurt), Puchka (pani puri).  
- For toddlers and young kids: Soft idli, plain rice with dal, mashed potatoes with a dash of mustard oil, and sweetened curd.  
- Vegetarian availability: Excellent; most stalls serve paneer rolls, vegetable kachori, and lentil‑based dishes, though fish‑centric items dominate the main menu.

## South Kolkata

**Key places:** Princep Ghat, Kalighat Temple, Science City, New Town Eco Park

**Princep Ghat:**  
- Entry: Free (boat ride ₹150 adult, ₹80 child 4‑12 yr)  
- Suitability: Flat promenade; **suitable for toddlers** (boat rides have safety rails) and **elderly** (wheelchair‑accessible ramps).  
- Best time: Sunset (5‑7 pm) for cooler air and city lights.  
- Seasonal closure: Boat services pause during heavy monsoon (July‑August) for safety.

**Kalighat Temple (Goddess Kali):**  
- Entry: Free (donation ₹30 adult, ₹15 child)  
- Suitability: Crowded inner sanctum; **not suitable for toddlers** (risk of pushing), **elderly** should use the side entrance with fewer steps.  
- Best time: Early morning (5‑7 am) before peak pilgrim flow.  
- Seasonal closure: None, but extra security on Navratri (Sept‑Oct).

**Science City:**  
- Entry: ₹120 adult, ₹80 child (3‑12 yr); **free for toddlers** under 3.  
- Suitability: Fully wheelchair‑accessible, interactive exhibits at low height – **excellent for toddlers** (with supervision) and **elderly**.  
- Best time: Late afternoon (3‑5 pm) when indoor exhibits are cooler.  
- Seasonal closure: Closed on Tuesdays.

**Trust issue for South Kolkata:**  
- Scam: “Temple donation bait” – some guides claim a mandatory “pandal fee” at Kalighat; it’s a voluntary donation.  
- Recommended approach: Politely decline any unsolicited “entry fee” and only donate at the official donation box.

**South Kolkata food:**  
- Signature dishes: Macher Muro (fried fish), Aloo Posto (potatoes in poppy seed paste), Shorshe Ilish (hilsa in mustard sauce), Rosogolla (spongy sweet).  
- For toddlers and young kids: Soft khichdi, boiled rice with a spoon of ghee, plain dal with soft roti, and sweetened yogurt.  
- Vegetarian availability: Strong; most restaurants serve vegetable curries, paneer bhaja, and lentil fritters, though fish dishes dominate menus.

## Sundarbans (South 24 Parganas)

**Key places:** Sundarbans National Park (Tiger Reserve), Sajnekhali Watch Tower, Dobanki Watch Tower

**Sundarbans National Park (Tiger Reserve):**  
- Entry: ₹300 adult, ₹150 child (5‑12 yr); **not suitable for toddlers** (boat ride lasts 2 hrs, no life‑vests for under‑3). **Elderly** should assess mobility; boat seats are low‑backed but stable.  
- Best time: Early morning (6‑9 am) for wildlife sightings and cooler humidity.  
- Seasonal closure: Closed during peak monsoon (July‑mid‑Sept) due to river flooding.

**Sajnekhali Watch Tower:**  
- Entry: ₹30 adult, ₹15 child; **suitable for toddlers** (short walk, railings), **elderly** can use the gentle ramp.  
- Best time: Late afternoon (4‑6 pm) when birds gather.  
- Seasonal closure: None, but rain can make the trail slippery.

**Trust issue for Sundarbans:**  
- Scam: “Unauthorized boat operators” – unlicensed boats quote low fares but lack safety gear and proper permits.  
- Recommended approach: Book only through the West Bengal Forest Department’s official website or reputable tour operators; verify boat registration numbers before boarding.

**Sundarbans food:**  
- Signature dishes: Macher Jhal (spicy fish), Paturi (fish wrapped in banana leaf), Chingri Malai Curry (prawn in coconut milk), Pitha (steamed rice cakes).  
- For toddlers and young kids: Soft rice porridge (khichuri), plain boiled potatoes, banana mash, and mild dal.  
- Vegetarian availability: Limited in core mangrove villages; however, many guesthouses serve vegetable pulao and lentil soup upon request.

## General Kolkata Notes

**Best time to visit:**  
- **Oct‑Feb:** Cool (15‑22 °C), festivals (Durga Puja) – all attractions open, boat rides in Sundarbans are smooth.  
- **Mar‑May:** Warm (28‑35 °C), occasional heat haze; early mornings best for outdoor sites.  
- **Jun‑Sept:** Monsoon (heavy rain, 200‑300 mm/month); river‑side promenades become slippery, Sundarbans boat services reduced, but indoor museums thrive.

**Transport pricing:**  
- **Taxi/cab:** Metered fares start at ₹25 for the first 2 km, then ₹13 per km; negotiate only for out‑station trips.  
- **Auto‑rickshaw:** Fixed‑rate for short hops (e.g., College Street to Park Street ≈ ₹30); for longer rides, ask for “meter” or a clear per‑km quote (≈ ₹10 per km).  
- **App‑based options:** Uber, Ola, and local app “Rapido” (bike‑share) operate city‑wide; surge pricing applies during peak hours (8‑10 am, 6‑9 pm).

**Dietary notes:**  
- Dominant cuisine: Bengali, heavy on mustard oil, poppy seed paste, and fresh river fish; coconut milk appears in coastal dishes.  
- Allergy risks: Coconut, mustard, and occasional pork (especially in “Mutton” dishes mislabeled).  
- Vegetarian options: Very good; most eateries serve paneer, mixed veg curries, and lentil dishes, though fish is the default protein.

**Medical:**  
- Nearest quality hospital: **Apollo Gleneagles Hospital, Alipore** (≈ 10 km from central Kolkata) – 24‑hour emergency, English‑speaking staff, and a well‑stocked pharmacy.  
- What to carry: Basic first‑aid kit, any prescription meds, a copy of vaccination records (especially for children), and a portable water‑purifier bottle for monsoon travel.
