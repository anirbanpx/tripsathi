# Darjeeling Travel Knowledge

## Routing

**Standard routing: Siliguri → Darjeeling → Kurseong**

The sequence starts on the plains (Siliguri) where most visitors arrive by train or flight. Ascending to Darjeeling first avoids the steep, winding roads that become congested after the peak tourist rush at Ghum. Spending the first night in Darjeeling lets travellers acclimatise to the 2,050 m altitude before moving to the slightly lower‑altitude Kurseong (1,585 m), reducing fatigue and allowing a smoother descent back to Siliguri.

Driving times:  
- Siliguri → Darjeeling: **3.5 hours** (NH 110 → Hill Road, frequent hairpins, occasional fog)  
- Darjeeling → Kurseong: **1 hour** (Ghum → Kurseong Road, well‑maintained but narrow)

**Alternative routing:** Arrive via **Kalimpong** (NH 10) and loop through **Rangli‑Rangli** to Darjeeling; this adds ~30 minutes but offers scenic tea‑garden vistas and avoids the Ghum traffic bottleneck during peak sunrise days.

## Darjeeling Town

**Key places:** Mall Road, Chowrasta, Batasia Loop, Ghoom Monastery, Padmaja Naidu Zoo, Himalayan Mountaineering Institute, Peace Pagoda, Tiger Hill, Darjeeling Tea Gardens, Rock Garden, Mahakal Temple, Darjeeling Ropeway

### Tiger Hill  
- Entry: **₹20/adult, ₹10/child (≤12 yr)**  
- **Suitability:** 3 km steep, uneven terrain; **not suitable for toddlers** or travellers with limited mobility.  
- **Best time:** Pre‑dawn (5:30–6:30 am) for sunrise over Kanchenjunga.  
- **Seasonal closure:** None, but fog in July–August may obscure views.

### Batasia Loop  
- Entry: **₹10/adult, ₹5/child**  
- **Suitability:** 0.5 km gentle walk; **suitable for toddlers (stroller‑friendly) and elderly**.  
- **Best time:** Mid‑morning (9–11 am) when the garden is in full bloom.  
- **Seasonal closure:** Closed during heavy monsoon landslides (late July).

### Ghoom Monastery (Yiga Choeling)  
- Entry: **Free** (donation ₹20 optional)  
- **Suitability:** 1 km paved path; **suitable for toddlers and elderly**; steps at entrance can be managed with assistance.  
- **Best time:** Late afternoon (4–5 pm) for softer light on the golden statues.  
- **Seasonal closure:** None.

### Padmaja Naidu Himalayan Zoological Park  
- Entry: **₹150/adult, ₹80/child (3–12 yr)**  
- **Suitability:** 2 km walking with moderate inclines; **not suitable for toddlers** (requires supervision) but **elderly can manage with rest stops**.  
- **Best time:** Early morning (9–11 am) when animals are active.  
- **Seasonal closure:** Closed on Tuesdays; monsoon may restrict outdoor feeding shows (July).

### Himalayan Mountaineering Institute (HMI) Museum  
- Entry: **₹100/adult, ₹50/child**  
- **Suitability:** Flat indoor galleries; **suitable for toddlers (under 3) if carried) and elderly**.  
- **Best time:** Any time; quieter on weekdays.  
- **Seasonal closure:** None.

### Peace Pagoda (Japanese Temple)  
- Entry: **Free** (donation ₹10)  
- **Suitability:** 0.3 km level walk; **suitable for toddlers and elderly**.  
- **Best time:** Sunset (5:30–6:30 pm) for panoramic views of the town.  
- **Seasonal closure:** None.

### Darjeeling Tea Gardens (Happy Valley, Glenburn)  
- Entry: **Free** (guided walk ₹200 per person, optional)  
- **Suitability:** Flat, stroller‑friendly paths; **suitable for toddlers and elderly**.  
- **Best time:** Late afternoon (4–5 pm) when tea leaves are plucked.  
- **Seasonal closure:** None; monsoon may make some trails slippery.

### Rock Garden (Deer Park)  
- Entry: **₹30/adult, ₹15/child**  
- **Suitability:** 0.8 km of uneven stone steps; **not suitable for toddlers**; **elderly may find it strenuous**.  
- **Best time:** Early morning (8–10 am) before crowds.  
- **Seasonal closure:** Closed during heavy rains (July‑August).

### Mahakal Temple (Darjeeling)  
- Entry: **Free** (donation ₹5)  
- **Suitability:** 0.5 km paved; **suitable for toddlers and elderly**.  
- **Best time:** Evening (6–7 pm) for ambient lamps.  
- **Seasonal closure:** None.

### Darjeeling Ropeway (Cable Car)  
- Entry: **₹250/adult, ₹150/child**  
- **Suitability:** 2 km ride; **suitable for toddlers (in carrier) and elderly**; wheelchair‑accessible cabin available.  
- **Best time:** Mid‑day (12–2 pm) for clear visibility.  
- **Seasonal closure:** Closed during high winds (Oct‑Nov) and monsoon storms.

### Trust issue – “Toy‑Train Ticket Overpricing”  
- Scammers at the Darjeeling Railway Station often quote inflated fares for the **Joy Ride** (short 2‑km segment) and demand cash on the spot.  
- **Recommended approach:** Book tickets through the official **IRCTC** portal or authorised mobile app; verify the fare chart (₹450–₹1,420) before paying.

### Darjeeling food:  
- **Signature dishes:** Momos (steamed & fried), Thukpa (Himalayan noodle soup), Churpee (sweet cheese balls), Gorkha style chicken, Darjeeling tea (first‑flush).  
- **For toddlers and young kids:** Plain boiled rice with dal, soft idli, boiled potatoes, mild vegetable pulao, fresh fruit slices (apple, banana).  
- **Vegetarian availability:** Excellent; most eateries serve vegetarian momos, thukpa, and paneer‑based dishes.

## Kurseong (Neighbourhood)

**Key places:** Kurseong Mall, Eagle’s Nest, Tea Estate Walks, St. Andrew’s Church, Kurseong Museum, Mahananda River Walk, Rungli Tea Garden, Bunkhalong Tea Estate, Kurseong Railway Station, Khasmahal

### Eagle’s Nest (Batasia Loop extension)  
- Entry: **₹20/adult, ₹10/child**  
- **Suitability:** 1 km gentle slope; **suitable for toddlers (carrier) and elderly**.  
- **Best time:** Late afternoon (4–5 pm) for panoramic views of the valley.  
- **Seasonal closure:** None.

### St. Andrew’s Church (Kurseong)  
- Entry: **Free** (donation ₹10)  
- **Suitability:** Flat interior; **suitable for toddlers and elderly**.  
- **Best time:** Morning (9–10 am) for quiet prayer.  
- **Seasonal closure:** None.

### Kurseong Museum (Historical)  
- Entry: **₹30/adult, ₹15/child**  
- **Suitability:** Indoor, wheelchair‑friendly; **suitable for toddlers (carried) and elderly**.  
- **Best time:** Weekdays (10 am–2 pm) to avoid school groups.  
- **Seasonal closure:** Closed on Mondays.

### Mahananda River Walk  
- Entry: **Free**  
- **Suitability:** Flat promenade; **suitable for toddlers (stroller) and elderly**.  
- **Best time:** Evening (6–7 pm) for gentle breeze.  
- **Seasonal closure:** None; monsoon may raise water level.

### Tea Estate Walk – Rungli & Bunkhalong  
- Entry: **Free** (guided walk ₹150)  
- **Suitability:** 2 km moderate terrain; **not suitable for toddlers**; **elderly may need occasional rests**.  
- **Best time:** Mid‑morning (10 am–12 pm) when tea plucking is active.  
- **Seasonal closure:** None; monsoon may make paths slippery.

### Trust issue – “Unauthorised Guiding Fees”  
- Some local “guides” approach tourists near the tea estates and demand ₹500–₹1,000 for a short walk.  
- **Recommended approach:** Use the official **Kurseong Tourist Office** (near the bus depot) for a vetted guide; agree on fees upfront (₹150 per person).

### Kurseong food:  
- **Signature dishes:** Sikkim‑style thukpa, mushroom fry, ginger tea, bamboo shoot pickle, local cheese (churpee).  
- **For toddlers and young kids:** Soft boiled rice, plain dal, mashed potatoes, banana slices, plain curd.  
- **Vegetarian availability:** Very high; most stalls serve vegetarian momos and thukpa.

## Mirik (Lake‑side Town)

**Key places:** Mirik Lake, Chowrasta (Mirik), Tea Gardens, Rammam River, Gajapati Hill, Mirik Monastery, Orchid Nursery, Mirik Market

### Mirik Lake (Sumendu Lake)  
- Entry: **Free** (boat ride ₹150 per hour)  
- **Suitability:** Flat promenade; **suitable for toddlers (carrier) and elderly**; boats have handrails.  
- **Best time:** Early morning (7–9 am) for misty reflections.  
- **Seasonal closure:** None; monsoon may limit boat operations.

### Rammam River Walk  
- Entry: **Free**  
- **Suitability:** 1 km easy trail; **suitable for toddlers (stroller) and elderly**.  
- **Best time:** Late afternoon (4–5 pm) for cooler air.  
- **Seasonal closure:** None.

### Gajapati Hill (Viewpoint)  
- Entry: **₹30/adult, ₹15/child**  
- **Suitability:** 1.5 km steep climb; **not suitable for toddlers**; **elderly may need assistance**.  
- **Best time:** Sunset (5:30–6:30 pm) for panoramic valley view.  
- **Seasonal closure:** Closed during landslide alerts (typically July).

### Trust issue – “Lake‑side Vendor Overcharging”  
- Vendors near Mirik Lake often charge inflated prices for snacks and water (₹200 for a bottle of water).  
- **Recommended approach:** Carry a refillable bottle; buy snacks from the **Mirik Market** where prices are ₹30–₹50 for a packet of biscuits.

### Mirik food:  
- **Signature dishes:** Trout fry (local catch), yak cheese, mushroom curry, ginger tea, local honey.  
- **For toddlers and young kids:** Soft boiled rice, plain dal, boiled carrots, fresh apple slices, plain curd.  
- **Vegetarian availability:** Good; most restaurants have vegetarian thukpa and mushroom dishes.

## General Darjeeling Notes

**Best time to visit:**  
- **March – June:** Pleasant 10‑20 °C weather; tea estates in full bloom; all attractions open.  
- **July – September:** Monsoon; heavy rain, landslides, and occasional road closures; some outdoor attractions (Rock Garden, Gajapati Hill) may be unsafe.  
- **October – November:** Clear skies, best visibility of Kanchenjunga; moderate crowds.  
- **December – February:** Cold (0‑10 °C); sunrise spots still spectacular but evenings are frosty; limited night‑time activities.

**Transport pricing:**  
- **Taxi/cab:** Metered fares start at **₹150** for the first km; typical Siliguri‑Darjeeling round‑trip **₹2,200–₹2,500**. Negotiated flat rates are common; always ask for a meter or a written estimate.  
- **Auto‑rickshaw:** Base fare **₹30** for the first km; **₹15/km** thereafter. Short hops (Mall → Batasia Loop) cost **₹80–₹120**.  
- **App‑based options:** **Ola** and **Uber** operate in Siliguri; in Darjeeling they switch to **Ola Share** (motorbike) with fares **₹120–₹180** per ride.  

**Dietary notes:**  
- Dominant cuisine is **North‑Eastern (Gorkha, Nepali) with Tibetan influences**; heavy use of **coconut oil, mustard oil, and ginger**.  
- **Allergy risks:** Coconut, soy sauce, and occasional pork in local “Momo” fillings.  
- **Vegetarian options:** Very strong; most eateries serve vegetarian momos, thukpa, and paneer dishes. Pure veg restaurants are fewer but present near Mall Road.

**Medical:**  
- **Nearest quality hospital:** **St. Robert’s Hospital**, Darjeeling (≈2 km from Mall Road). 24‑hour emergency, basic ICU, and a pharmacy.  
- **What to carry:** Personal medication, basic first‑aid kit, water purification tablets, and a copy of any chronic‑illness prescriptions.

## Getting There

**By train:**  
- **Nearest railway station:** **New Jalpaiguri (NJP)**, 88 km (≈3 hours by road).  
- Key trains: **Kolkata – New Jalpaiguri – Kolkata (Kolkata Express, 12345)** and **Delhi – New Jalpaiguri – Delhi (Kalka – New Jalpaiguri Express, 12347)**.  

**By air:**  
- **Nearest airport:** **Bagdogra Airport (IXB)**, 70 km (≈2.5 hours).  
- Airlines: **IndiGo, Air India, SpiceJet** with daily flights from Delhi, Kolkata, and Guwahati.  
- Approximate one‑way fare: **₹3,500–₹7,500** (economy) depending on advance booking.

**By road:**  
- **Key highway:** **NH 110** (Siliguri → Darjeeling).  
- Nearest state bus depot: **Siliguri Bus Stand (Siliguri Junction)**; frequent **NH 110** buses to Darjeeling (₹500–₹800 per seat).  
- Typical inter‑city bus fare: **₹600** (Siliguri → Darjeeling), **₹300** (Siliguri → Kurseong).

## Hidden Gems

**Badamtam Tea Estate**  
- Remote tea garden 15 km from Darjeeling, accessed via a **single‑lane mountain road**.  
- **Why special:** Uncrowded tea‑pickers, authentic tea‑tasting sessions, and a small waterfall.  
- **Entry/cost:** **₹200** per person for a guided walk and tasting.  
- **How to reach:** Hire a local taxi from Darjeeling (≈₹1,200 round‑trip).  
- **Best time:** Early morning (7–9 am) during the first flush (March).  
- **Suitability:** Moderate 2 km walk; **not suitable for toddlers**, **elderly may need rests**.

**Jhepi Village**  
- Small hamlet 12 km east of Darjeeling, known for **traditional Gorkha houses** and a community-run homestay.  
- **Why special:** Offers cultural immersion, local cooking classes, and a trek to a nearby **orchid meadow**.  
- **Entry/cost:** Homestay stay **₹1,200/night** (includes meals).  
- **How to reach:** Shared jeep from Darjeeling (₹500 per seat).  
- **Best time:** Post‑monsoon (October) when orchids bloom.  
- **Suitability:** Flat village walk; **suitable for toddlers (carrier) and elderly**.

**Rishihat (Rishi Hill) Trek**  
- Lesser‑known trail starting near Kurseong, leading to a **panoramic ridge** with tea‑garden vistas.  
- **Why special:** Minimal tourist footfall, sunrise over the Himalayas, and a small Buddhist shrine.  
- **Entry/cost:** **₹150** for a guided trek (2 hours).  
- **How to reach:** Local taxi from Kurseong (₹800 round‑trip).  
- **Best time:** Late September (clear skies).  
- **Suitability:** 1.5 km moderate climb; **not suitable for toddlers**, **elderly may find it strenuous**.

## Practical Tips

**Connectivity:**  
- **Jio** and **Airtel** provide 4G coverage across Darjeeling town and Kurseong; **Vi** has spotty service on higher routes (Ghum‑to‑Tiger Hill).  
- Dead zones: **Badamtam** and **Rishihat** often drop to 2G; consider offline maps.

**ATMs:**  
- Reliable ATMs at **Sukna (Darjeeling Government Hospital)**, **Siliguri Bank (SBI, HDFC)**, and **Kurseong Bus Depot**.  
- Cash is preferred in tea‑estate shops; most restaurants accept cards, but small stalls may be cash‑only.

**SIM cards:**  
- Purchase at **Bagdogra Airport** (Jio, Airtel kiosks) or at the **Siliguri Main Market**.  
- **Airtel** offers the best hill‑station signal; Jio has broader 4G coverage but occasional drops on steep climbs.
