# Khajuraho Travel Knowledge

## Routing

**Standard routing: Jhansi → Khajuraho → Panna → Jabalpur**

Starting from Jhansi (major rail hub) allows travelers to reach Khajuraho via the well‑maintained NH75, minimizing fatigue before the temple circuit. After exploring the monuments, heading south to Panna National Park on NH39 avoids back‑tracking and aligns with the natural flow of wildlife‑safari timings. The final leg to Jabalpur on NH30 provides access to a major airport and advanced medical facilities.

Driving times:  
- Jhansi → Khajuraho: 4 h (NH75, flat terrain, occasional cattle crossings)  
- Khajuraho → Panna: 2 h 30 m (NH39, winding through limestone hills)  
- Panna → Jabalpur: 4 h 15 m (NH30, well‑paved, passes through Sagar)

**Alternative routing:** Enter from Gwalior via NH44 → Khajuraho → Panna → Jabalpur. This adds ~30 km but offers a smoother highway segment from Gwalior for those flying into Gwalior Airport.

## Khajuraho Group of Monuments

**Key places:** Western Group (Kandariya Mahadev, Lakshmana, Vishnu), Eastern Group (Parsvanatha, Jain temples), Archaeological Museum, Sound and Light Show venue.

**Kandariya Mahadev Temple (Western Group):**  
- Entry: ₹200 adult, ₹100 child (3‑12 yrs), free for children under 3.  
- Suitability: 3 km of uneven stone steps and narrow passages; **not suitable for toddlers** and **caution advised for elderly with limited mobility**.  
- Best time of day: Early morning (7:30–9:30 am) for cooler temperatures and softer light for photography.  
- Seasonal closure: Temple closes for worship from 12:00 pm to 2:00 pm daily; otherwise open year‑round.

**Trust issue or pricing trap for this area:** Unauthorized “guide” agents near the museum often charge ₹500–₹800 for a “full‑day” guide service, inflating the standard ₹200 guide fee.  
- Recommended approach: Hire a licensed guide at the official guide desk (₹200 adult) or use the free audio guide app provided by the ASI.

**Khajuraho food:**  
- Signature dishes: **Bhutte ki kees** (spiced grated corn), **Dal bafla** (baked wheat balls with lentil gravy), **Poha** (flattened rice with peanuts).  
- For toddlers and young kids: **Idli**, **plain rice with dal**, **soft boiled potatoes**.  
- Vegetarian availability: Excellent; most stalls serve pure vegetarian fare, and meat dishes are rare.

## Panna National Park & Tiger Reserve

**Key places:** Pandavgarh Fort, Pandav Caves, Panna Tiger Reserve, Van Vihar (nature trail).

**Safari in Panna Tiger Reserve:**  
- Entry: ₹200 adult, ₹100 child (3‑12 yrs); children under 3 ride free but must be seated on an adult’s lap.  
- Suitability: Safari jeeps traverse rough tracks; **not suitable for toddlers** (risk of jolts) and **elderly travellers should ensure they are comfortable with uneven seating**.  
- Best time of day: Dawn safari (6:00–9:00 am) for maximum animal activity and cooler climate.  
- Seasonal closure: Park closed during monsoon (July – September) due to flooding of water bodies and road damage.

**Trust issue or pricing trap for this area:** Many local operators advertise “all‑inclusive” safari packages at ₹2,500‑₹3,000 per person, but the official park fee is only ₹200. The extra amount often covers unlicensed vehicle hire and inflated guide fees.  
- Recommended approach: Book directly at the Panna Forest Department office or through the official website; verify the vehicle registration and guide badge before boarding.

**Panna food:**  
- Signature dishes: **Mawa ki sabzi** (soft paneer in creamy gravy), **Bhutta** (roasted corn with lime), **Sabudana khichdi** (tapioca pearls with peanuts).  
- For toddlers and young kids: **Plain boiled potatoes**, **curd rice**, **soft khichdi** (rice‑lentil porridge).  
- Vegetarian availability: Good; most eateries offer vegetarian thalis, though some popular dishes include fish or game meat—ask for “veg only” to avoid surprise.

## General Khajuraho Notes

**Best time to visit:**  
- **October – March:** Cool (10‑25 °C), festivals (Khajuraho Dance Festival in February) boost cultural experiences; all monuments fully accessible.  
- **April – June:** Hot (30‑40 °C); early morning visits recommended to avoid heat; water scarcity may affect rural eateries.  
- **July – September:** Monsoon; heavy rain can cause temporary closure of the Eastern Group temples and the park; mosquito activity rises—use repellents.

**Transport pricing:**  
- **Taxi/cab:** From Khajuraho Airport to the Western Group – ₹500‑₹800 (metered or fixed, negotiate before boarding).  
- **Auto‑rickshaw:** ₹12‑₹15 per km; short hops between temples often charged per ride (≈₹30‑₹50).  
- **App‑based options:** Uber and Ola operate in Khajuraho city centre; typical fare for 5 km is ₹250‑₹300.  
- **Shared jeep (local):** ₹200 per person for a round‑trip covering both temple groups; seats fill quickly in peak season.

**Dietary notes:**  
- Dominant cuisine is North‑Indian with liberal use of ghee and moderate spice levels. Coconut oil appears mainly in sweets (e.g., coconut laddoo). Pork is not a traditional ingredient; most meat dishes are lamb or chicken.  
- Vegetarian options: Abundant; most restaurants serve a full veg thali (roti, dal, sabzi, rice, sweet). Ensure “no onion/garlic” if following Jain or specific religious restrictions.

**Medical:**  
- Nearest quality hospital: **Madhya Pradesh State Government Hospital, Khajuraho** (≈5 km from the temple complex, 24 h emergency services).  
- For serious cases, the **Madhav Medical College Hospital, Jabalpur** (≈150 km) offers tertiary care.  
- Recommended to carry a personal first‑aid kit: basic pain relievers, antihistamines, oral rehydration salts, any regular prescriptions, and a copy of vaccination records (especially for tetanus).
