# Khajuraho Travel Knowledge

## Routing

**Standard routing: Delhi → Jhansi → Khajuraho**

Starting from Delhi, the drive to Jhansi on NH44 (formerly NH3) is a long but well‑maintained highway with frequent fuel stations and rest stops. From Jhansi, NH75 (Jhansi‑Khajuraho Road) climbs gently through the Bundelkhand plateau, allowing travelers to reach Khajuraho before sunset and avoid night‑time fatigue on winding rural stretches.

Driving times:  
- Delhi → Jhansi: **≈ 5 hours** (NH44, toll‑free sections)  
- Jhansi → Khajuraho: **≈ 3 hours** (NH75, two‑lane highway, occasional cattle crossings)

**Alternative routing:** Fly into Khajuraho Airport (≈ 1 hour from the town) and rent a car or hire a driver; for those preferring rail, take the train to Satna (≈ 6 hours) and then a 2‑hour road transfer via State Highway 14.

## Western Group of Temples

**Key places:** Kandariya Mahadev, Lakshmana, Vishwanath, Parvati, Brahma Temple (offbeat)

**Kandariya Mahadev Temple:**  
- Entry: **₹250 / adult Indian**, **₹700 / foreigner** (no separate child charge)  
- Suitability: **Not suitable for toddlers** – steep steps and 30 m high shikhara; **Elderly travellers should use the hand‑rail at the base and avoid the upper balcony**.  
- Best time: Early morning (7:30 am–9:00 am) when the sun illuminates the western façade.  
- Seasonal closure: Closed on **Wednesdays** for maintenance.

**Lakshmana Temple:**  
- Entry: **₹250 / adult Indian**, **₹700 / foreigner**  
- Suitability: **Toddler‑friendly** only for brief exterior viewing; interior has narrow corridors and low‑light areas, making it **challenging for elderly with mobility issues**.  
- Best time: Mid‑day (11:30 am–1:30 pm) to catch the inner sanctum’s light play.  
- Seasonal closure: Open all days except Wednesday.

**Vishwanath Temple:**  
- Entry: **₹250 / adult Indian**, **₹700 / foreigner**  
- Suitability: **Flat ground, good for toddlers in a stroller**; however, the stone steps at the entrance can be slippery after rain – **elderly should use a cane**.  
- Best time: Late afternoon (4:00 pm–5:30 pm) for a cooler visit.  
- Seasonal closure: None; open daily.

**Parvati Temple:**  
- Entry: **₹250 / adult Indian**, **₹700 / foreigner**  
- Suitability: **Small courtyard, easy for toddlers**; interior ceiling height is low, so **elderly with back problems should avoid prolonged stays**.  
- Best time: Sunrise (6:30 am–8:00 am) when the temple faces east.  
- Seasonal closure: Closed on Wednesdays.

**Brahma Temple (offbeat):**  
- Entry: **₹250 / adult Indian**, **₹700 / foreigner**  
- Suitability: **Quiet and less crowded, suitable for toddlers**; a short walk of 200 m from the main road, **no steep climbs** – ideal for elderly travellers.  
- Best time: Early evening (5:00 pm–6:30 pm) for a tranquil ambience.  
- Seasonal closure: Same as other temples (closed Wednesdays).

**Trust issue for Western Group:**  
- **Overpriced “guided entry” offers** from street vendors claim “all‑temple passes” at inflated rates (₹800‑₹1,200).  
- Recommended approach: Purchase the official **single‑day ticket at the ASI ticket counter** (₹250 Indian) and, if a guide is needed, hire a **registered ASI guide** inside the complex for ₹300 / hour.

**Western Group food:**  
- Signature dishes: **Dal Bafla**, **Bhutte Ka Kees** (spiced corn), **Kachori** with tamarind chutney.  
- For toddlers and young kids: **Plain rice with dal**, **soft idli**, **boiled potatoes (aloo)**.  
- Vegetarian availability: **All dishes are vegetarian; meat is rarely served in the town market**.

## Eastern Group of Temples

**Key places:** Chaturbhuj, Devi Jagadambi, Jain Temple, Javari Temple, Vaman Temple

**Chaturbhuj Temple:**  
- Entry: **₹250 / adult Indian**, **₹700 / foreigner**  
- Suitability: **Large courtyard, flat terrain – excellent for toddlers**; the interior houses a massive 22‑ton Shiva lingam, requiring **no stairs for elderly**.  
- Best time: Mid‑morning (9:30 am–11:00 am) when the temple is less crowded.  
- Seasonal closure: Closed Wednesdays.

**Devi Jagadambi Temple:**  
- Entry: **₹250 / adult Indian**, **₹700 / foreigner**  
- Suitability: **Narrow entrance steps (≈ 1 m rise) make it **unsuitable for toddlers in strollers** and **elderly with joint pain**.  
- Best time: Late afternoon (4:30 pm–5:30 pm) for the golden‑hour glow on the shikhara.  
- Seasonal closure: None.

**Jain Temple (Shantinath):**  
- Entry: **₹250 / adult Indian**, **₹700 / foreigner**  
- Suitability: **Flat marble floor, wheelchair‑friendly ramps installed (2024 upgrade)** – **ideal for both toddlers and elderly**.  
- Best time: Early evening (5:00 pm–6:30 pm) for a peaceful ambience.  
- Seasonal closure: Closed on **Wednesdays**.

**Javari Temple (offbeat):**  
- Entry: **₹250 / adult Indian**, **₹700 / foreigner**  
- Suitability: **Quiet, minimal footfall; steps are low (≈ 0.5 m) – manageable for toddlers with assistance and elderly with a walking stick**.  
- Best time: Sunrise (6:00 am–7:30 am) for soft lighting on the carvings.  
- Seasonal closure: Open daily.

**Vaman Temple (offbeat):**  
- Entry: **₹250 / adult Indian**, **₹700 / foreigner**  
- Suitability: **Optical‑illusion façade; interior is cramped, so **not recommended for toddlers**; elderly can view the exterior only.  
- Best time: Mid‑day (12:00 pm–1:30 pm) when the illusion is most visible.  
- Seasonal closure: None.

**Trust issue for Eastern Group:**  
- **“Free photo” scams** – vendors near the temple gates offer “free photography” and later demand ₹500‑₹800 for “editing”.  
- Recommended approach: Use your own smartphone or hire a **licensed photographer** from the ASI office (₹250 / hour) and politely decline unsolicited offers.

**Eastern Group food:**  
- Signature dishes: **Poha (flattened rice) with sev**, **Muthiya (steamed gram flour dumplings)**, **Gulab Jamun** (sweet).  
- For toddlers and young kids: **Soft khichdi**, **plain boiled rice**, **steamed carrot sticks**.  
- Vegetarian availability: **Fully vegetarian; most stalls serve only vegetarian fare**.

## Nearby Attractions

**Key places:** Beni Sagar Dam, Rani Durgawati Museum, Panna National Park, Brahma Temple (already listed), Badi Sagar (Grand Canyon of India)

**Beni Sagar Dam:**  
- Entry: **₹25 / adult Indian**, **₹300 / foreigner** (boat ride extra ₹50)  
- Suitability: **Shallow promenade, wheelchair‑friendly paths – good for toddlers and elderly**.  
- Best time: Late afternoon (4:00 pm–6:00 pm) for sunset reflections.  
- Seasonal closure: Closed on **Wednesdays**.

**Rani Durgawati Museum:**  
- Entry: **₹10 / adult Indian**, **₹250 / foreigner**  
- Suitability: **Small galleries, low steps, air‑conditioned – suitable for both toddlers (short visits) and elderly**.  
- Best time: Morning (9:30 am–11:30 am) before the heat builds.  
- Seasonal closure: Open daily except Wednesday.

**Panna National Park (≈ 45 km):**  
- Entry: **₹200 / adult Indian**, **₹600 / foreigner** (safari vehicle)  
- Suitability: **Safari jeeps have low seats; toddlers should be in carriers; elderly can manage the short rides but avoid long walks**.  
- Best time: Early morning (5:30 am–9:30 am) for wildlife spotting.  
- Seasonal closure: **Closed during monsoon (July‑September)**.

**Badi Sagar (Grand Canyon of India):**  
- Entry: **Free** (parking ₹20)  
- Suitability: **Uneven rocky trails (≈ 2 km) – **not suitable for toddlers**; elderly with good stamina can manage with walking sticks.  
- Best time: Early winter (December‑January) when water flow is low and views are clear.  
- Seasonal closure: None, but access may be restricted after heavy rains.

**Trust issue for Nearby Attractions:**  
- **“Unlicensed safari operators”** at Panna park often quote inflated vehicle fees and provide unsafe, overcrowded jeeps.  
- Recommended approach: Book **through the official Madhya Pradesh Forest Department portal** or via a reputable tour operator; verify the vehicle registration plate before boarding.

**Nearby attractions food:**  
- Signature dishes: **Madhya Pradesh thali**, **Sabudana Khichdi**, **Mutton Curry** (optional for non‑vegetarians).  
- For toddlers and young kids: **Plain roti with ghee**, **soft boiled egg**, **sweet lassi**.  
- Vegetarian availability: **Thali includes at least three vegetarian items; veg‑only options are abundant**.

## General Khajuraho Notes

**Best time to visit:**  
- **October – March:** Pleasant 10‑25 °C, all temples open, festivals (Khajuraho Dance Festival) boost cultural vibe.  
- **April – June:** Hot (30‑40 °C); early morning visits recommended; some interior galleries close at 1 pm to protect artifacts.  
- **July – September:** Monsoon; water seepage can make stone carvings slippery; **Panna National Park closed**, Beni Sagar boat rides limited.  
- **December – January:** Cool evenings, occasional fog; sunrise at Parvati Temple is spectacular but visibility may be reduced.

**Transport pricing:**  
- **Taxi/cab:** Metered fares start at **₹12 / km**; typical airport‑to‑town ride ≈ 20 km = **₹240**. Negotiated flat rates are common; always ask for a meter or a written estimate.  
- **Auto‑rickshaw:** Fixed fare **₹15 / km**; short hops within town (≤ 5 km) cost **₹75‑₹100**. Auto‑rickshaws do not use meters; confirm fare before boarding.  
- **App‑based options:** **Ola and Uber** operate in Khajuraho with limited fleet; base fare **₹120** plus ₹10 / km. Expect surge pricing during festivals.

**Dietary notes:**  
- Dominant cuisine: **Bundelkhand** – heavy use of wheat, gram flour, and mustard oil.  
- Allergy risks: **Coconut oil** is common in sweets; **poppy seeds** appear in some desserts; **pork** is rarely served, but occasional non‑vegetarian stalls may offer goat.  
- Vegetarian options: **Abundant**; most restaurants and street stalls are vegetarian‑friendly. Ensure to ask for “**sabzi‑only**” if you avoid dairy.

**Medical:**  
- **District Hospital Khajuraho** (≈ 5 km from town centre) offers 24‑hour emergency services, basic surgery, and a pharmacy.  
- **Private clinic “Sanjivani Health Centre”** (≈ 2 km) provides pediatric care and is English‑speaking.  
- Carry a **personal first‑aid kit**, any chronic medication, and a copy of your health insurance card.

## Getting There

**By train:**  
- Nearest railway station: **Khajuraho Railway Station (KURJ)**, 3 km from town.  
- Key trains: **Jabalpur‑Khajuraho Express (16555)** from Jabalpur (≈ 4 h) and **Jhansi‑Khajuraho Passenger (51813)** from Jhansi (≈ 3 h). Both run daily.  

**By air:**  
- **Khajuraho Airport (HJR)**, 5 km from the town.  
- Airlines: **Air India**, **IndiGo**, and **SpiceJet** operate daily flights from Delhi (≈ 1 h 15 min) and Varanasi (≈ 1 h).  
- Approximate one‑way fare (economy, 2025): **₹3,500 – ₹6,500** from Delhi; **₹2,800 – ₹4,500** from Varanasi.

**By road:**  
- **National Highway 75** (Jhansi‑Khajuraho) is the primary route.  
- Nearest state bus depot: **Khajuraho Bus Stand**, 2 km from town centre; state‑run buses from **Jhansi, Satna, and Varanasi** run every 2‑3 hours, fare **₹250 – ₹400**.  
- Private inter‑city buses (e.g., **VRL Travels**) offer AC coaches at **₹350 – ₹550**.

## Hidden Gems

**Brahma Temple (offbeat, not in main temple circuit):**  
- Why special: Dedicated to Brahma, the only temple of its kind in the region; quiet, minimal crowds, and a unique four‑armed statue.  
- Entry: **₹250 / adult Indian**, **₹700 / foreigner**; no additional charges.  
- How to reach: Walk 200 m north from the main Western Group parking lot; signposted on footpath.  
- Best time: Early evening (5:30 pm–6:30 pm) when the setting sun casts a warm glow on the stone.  
- Suitability: **Flat ground, stroller‑friendly, wheelchair ramp present – ideal for toddlers and elderly**.

**Javari Temple (Eastern fringe):**  
- Why special: Lesser‑visited, serene atmosphere, intricate eastern wall carvings rarely photographed.  
- Entry: Same as other temples (**₹250 / adult Indian**).  
- How to reach: Take a local auto from the main market heading east; 3 km, drop at the small signboard.  
- Best time: Sunrise (6:00 am–7:30 am) for soft lighting and cooler temperatures.  
- Suitability: **Low steps (≈ 0.5 m)** – toddlers need assistance; elderly can manage with a walking stick.

**Badi Sagar (Grand Canyon of India):**  
- Why special: A dramatic sandstone gorge formed by the Ken River, offering trekking and photography opportunities away from the temple crowds.  
- Entry: **Free** (parking ₹20).  
- How to reach: Hire a local driver from Khajuraho (≈ 45 km, 1 hour) via State Highway 14.  
- Best time: Early winter (December‑January) when water levels are low and the gorge is fully exposed.  
- Suitability: **Uneven rocky trails** – not recommended for toddlers; elderly with good balance can enjoy short walks with a trekking pole.

## Practical Tips

**Connectivity:**  
- **Jio, Airtel, and Vi** provide 4G coverage throughout Khajuraho town; signal strength drops near Beni Sagar and Badi Sagar, where **only Jio** maintains intermittent 3G. Carry a portable hotspot if you need reliable internet for navigation.

**ATMs:**  
- **SBI ATM** at the Khajuraho Market (near the main bus stand).  
- **HDFC ATM** inside the Khajuraho Shopping Complex (≈ 1 km from temples).  
- Cash is preferred for small stalls and temple entry; most larger hotels accept cards, but **always keep ₹2,000‑₹5,000 in cash** for emergencies.

**SIM cards:**  
- Purchase a **pre‑paid Jio or Airtel SIM** at the airport kiosk or at the **Khajuraho Railway Station kiosk**. Jio offers the best rural coverage; Airtel provides slightly better data speeds in town. Bring passport for KYC; activation is immediate (within 30 minutes).
