# Pushkar Travel Knowledge

## Routing

**Standard routing: Delhi → Ajmer → Pushkar**

The sequence minimizes back‑tracking and leverages the high‑speed NH 48 corridor from Delhi to Ajmer, then a short state‑highway stretch to Pushkar. Ajmer serves as the nearest rail/air hub, allowing travelers to rest before the final 15 km leg, which reduces fatigue on the winding approach to the lake town.

Driving times:  
- Delhi → Ajmer: ~6 hours (NH 48, well‑maintained, toll plazas at Neemrana & Kishangarh)  
- Ajmer → Pushkar: ~0.5 hour (NH 48 → State Highway 58, single carriageway, occasional cattle crossing)

**Alternative routing:** Delhi → Jaipur → Pushkar (≈7 hours via NH 48 to Jaipur, then NH 48 → NH 58 to Pushkar). This route is useful when Jaipur is a planned stop for heritage sightseeing or when traffic on the Delhi‑Ajmer stretch is heavy.

## Pushkar Town (Lake & Bazaar)

**Key places:** Pushkar Lake, Brahma Temple, Pushkar Market, Sunset Point, Ghushmeshwar Temple

**Pushkar Lake:**  
- Entry: Free (₹0) – modest parking fee ₹20 for cars.  
- Suitability: 2 km flat promenade, well‑maintained pathways; suitable for toddlers in a stroller and elderly travellers with limited mobility.  
- Best time of day: Early morning (5:30–7:30 am) for sunrise rituals and cooler temperatures.  
- Seasonal closure: None, but water levels drop sharply in peak summer (May‑June), making the lake edge muddy.

**Brahma Temple:**  
- Entry: ₹30 adult, ₹15 child (3–12 yr). No entry for toddlers under 3.  
- Suitability: 200 m of steps and a 3 km uneven courtyard; not recommended for toddlers or elderly with walking difficulties.  
- Best time of day: Late afternoon (4–5 pm) to avoid the mid‑day heat and to catch the aarti.  
- Seasonal closure: Closed on Tuesdays (maintenance) and on major Hindu festivals when crowd density spikes.

**Pushkar Market (Bazaars):**  
- Entry: Free.  
- Suitability: Narrow lanes, occasional uneven cobbles; toddlers should be carried, and elderly travellers may need a supportive walking stick.  
- Best time of day: Mid‑morning (10 am–12 pm) when shops are fully stocked but crowds are manageable.  
- Seasonal closure: None, but the market is exceptionally crowded during the Pushkar Camel Fair (late Oct‑early Nov).

**Trust issue – “Handicraft price inflation”:**  
- Scam: Vendors often quote inflated rates for “authentic Rajasthani” items, especially to foreign tourists.  
- Recommended approach: Agree on a price before picking an item, ask for a written quote, and compare with at least two other stalls. Use a trusted local guide or ask the tourist information desk for a price list.

**Pushkar food:**  
- Signature dishes: Dal Baati Churma, Gatte ki Sabzi, Ker Sangri, Ghevar (sweet), Lassi.  
- For toddlers and young kids: Soft khichdi, plain boiled rice with dal, idli (steamed rice cakes), butter‑roti, mashed potatoes.  
- Vegetarian availability: Excellent; most eateries are strictly vegetarian, with a few offering paneer‑based non‑veg substitutes. Vegan options are limited but can be requested (e.g., “no ghee”).

## Savitri Temple & Brahma Temple Area

**Key places:** Savitri Temple, Brahma Temple, Sunset Point, Pushkar Museum

**Savitri Temple:**  
- Entry: ₹20 adult, ₹10 child (3–12 yr). Free for toddlers under 3.  
- Suitability: Steep 300‑step climb; unsuitable for toddlers and elderly travellers with knee or back issues.  
- Best time of day: Early evening (6–7 pm) for panoramic sunset views over the lake.  
- Seasonal closure: Closed on Mondays (maintenance) and during heavy monsoon showers (July‑August) due to slippery steps.

**Sunset Point (near Savitri Temple):**  
- Entry: Free (₹0).  
- Suitability: 1 km gentle slope; fully accessible for strollers and walkers with limited mobility.  
- Best time of day: 6:30–7:30 pm during winter months for clear skies; in summer, visit after 7 pm to avoid heat haze.  
- Seasonal closure: None, but monsoon fog can obscure views in July.

**Trust issue – “Temple donation over‑charging”:**  
- Scam: Some staff request “mandatory” donations for entry or aarti tickets, inflating amounts beyond the official ₹30 fee.  
- Recommended approach: Carry exact change, ask for a receipt, and verify the official fee posted at the entrance. If a staff member insists on extra payment, politely decline and move to the next gate.

**Savitri food:**  
- Signature dishes: Laal maas (spicy meat curry – note: non‑veg), Bajra roti, Mirchi bada, Thandai.  
- For toddlers and young kids: Plain bajra roti with ghee, boiled carrots, soft curd rice, plain roti with ghee.  
- Vegetarian availability: High; most stalls serve vegetarian versions of the same dishes, and non‑veg items are clearly labeled.

## Pushkar Desert Camp (Rajasthan Desert Experience)

**Key places:** Camel Safari, Desert Camp (overnight), Night Bazaar

**Camel Safari (Dune Ride):**  
- Entry: ₹500 adult, ₹300 child (4–12 yr). No entry for toddlers under 4.  
- Suitability: 3 km ride over soft dunes; not suitable for toddlers, and elderly travellers with balance issues should avoid.  
- Best time of day: Sunset (5:30–7 pm) for cooler temperatures and dramatic lighting.  
- Seasonal closure: Closed during intense monsoon (July‑early August) when dunes become slippery.

**Desert Camp (overnight stay):**  
- Entry: ₹1,200 per person for basic tent with shared bathroom (includes dinner).  
- Suitability: Tent flooring is sand‑filled; suitable for children over 3 with supervision, but elderly travellers may find the sand uncomfortable; recommend a cot or mattress pad.  
- Best time of day: Arrive by 4 pm to avoid late‑day heat; camp activities (folk dance, bonfire) start after sunset.  
- Seasonal closure: Camp operators often shut down in peak summer (May‑June) due to extreme heat (>45 °C).

**Trust issue – “Unregulated camel ride pricing”:**  
- Scam: Drivers may quote a low price upfront, then add hidden fees for “saddle” or “photo” charges.  
- Recommended approach: Agree on a total price before mounting, confirm that the amount includes the ride and any photos, and request a written receipt. Use a reputable operator recommended by the Pushkar Tourist Office.

**Desert food:**  
- Signature dishes: Ker Sangri khichdi, Rajasthani kadhi, Bajra ladoo, fresh buttermilk.  
- For toddlers and young kids: Soft boiled rice with a spoonful of ghee, plain roti, mashed sweet potatoes, plain yogurt.  
- Vegetarian availability: Fully vegetarian; the desert camp typically serves only vegetarian fare, with dairy‑based dishes.

## General Pushkar Notes

**Best time to visit:**  
- **October – February:** Cool (10‑25 °C), clear skies, all attractions open; peak of Pushkar Camel Fair (late Oct) brings crowds but vibrant cultural shows.  
- **March – April:** Warm (25‑35 °C), early summer; lake area can be hot, but festivals like Holi add color.  
- **May – June:** Very hot (30‑45 °C), water levels recede, desert camps may close; advisable to limit outdoor activities to early morning/evening.  
- **July – September:** Monsoon (heavy showers, occasional flooding of low‑lying streets); some temple steps become slippery, and market access may be restricted.

**Transport pricing:**  
- **Taxi from Ajmer to Pushkar:** Fixed fare ₹500–₹600 for a standard sedan; meter rarely used on this short stretch.  
- **Auto‑rickshaw within Pushkar:** ₹12‑₹15 per km; negotiate before boarding, or ask for the meter to be switched on (many are off by default).  
- **App‑based rides (Uber/Ola):** Operate from Ajmer and Jaipur; in Pushkar coverage is spotty, but rides can be booked for the 15 km Ajmer leg and then switched to local autos.

**Dietary notes:**  
- Dominant cuisine: Rajasthani (ghee‑rich, millets, legumes). Common allergens: dairy (ghee, paneer, curd) and nuts (almonds in sweets). Coconut oil is rarely used, and pork is virtually absent.  
- Vegetarian options: Abundant; most restaurants are fully vegetarian, with clear labeling for non‑veg items. Vegan travelers should request “no ghee” and “no dairy” – many kitchens can accommodate.

**Medical:**  
- Nearest quality hospital: **Mahaveer Hospital, Ajmer** (≈15 km, 24‑hour emergency, well‑equipped ICU).  
- Pushkar also hosts a small **Pushkar Primary Health Centre** (₹0‑₹200 for basic consultation) but limited to minor ailments.  
- Recommended to carry a personal first‑aid kit (band‑aids, antiseptic, basic pain relievers), any chronic medication, and a copy of prescriptions for travelers with diabetes or hypertension.
