# Mumbai Travel Knowledge

## Routing

**Standard routing: Mumbai Airport → South Mumbai (Colaba) → Bandra (West) → Navi Mumbai (East)**  

The sequence lets travellers first soak up the historic core where most major sights cluster, then move west to the vibrant Bandra‑BKC area before crossing the Sion‑Panvel Expressway to the greener eastern suburbs. This order minimises back‑tracking, avoids the worst rush‑hour congestion, and spreads fatigue by alternating dense urban walking with easier suburban drives.

Driving times:  
- Mumbai Airport → South Mumbai: ~45 min (NH 48 / Airport Road)  
- South Mumbai → Bandra: ~30 min (Western Express Highway)  
- Bandra → Navi Mumbai: ~45 min (Sion‑Panvel Expressway)

**Alternative routing:** Arrive by train at Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus, then use the local suburban network to loop clockwise—first to Bandra via Western Line, then south to Colaba by local train and ferry, and finally east to Navi Mumbai via Harbour Line. This avoids road traffic and showcases Mumbai’s iconic local‑train experience.

## South Mumbai (Colaba, Fort, Marine Drive)

**Key places:** Gateway of India, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus, Marine Drive, Kala Ghoda, Elephanta Caves (ferry)

**Gateway of India:**  
- Entry: ₹0 (free)  
- Suitability: Flat paved promenade, wheelchair‑friendly ramps; suitable for toddlers (stroller‑compatible) and elderly (no steep steps).  
- Best time of day: 06:00‑08:00 hrs to beat crowds and heat.  
- Seasonal closure: None, but monsoon (June‑Sept) can make the surrounding promenade slippery.

**Trust issue or pricing trap for this area:**  
- Scam: Airport‑to‑gateway prepaid taxis often quote inflated flat rates (₹1,200‑₹1,500) instead of the regulated meter fare.  
- Recommended approach: Use the official prepaid‑taxi kiosk at the airport or book an app‑based ride (Uber/Ola) where the fare is displayed before confirming.

**South Mumbai food:**  
- Signature dishes: Vada pav, Pav bhaji, Misal pav, Bhelpuri, Bombay‑style fish fry.  
- For toddlers and young kids: Soft idli, plain dhokla, boiled rice with dal, plain buttered toast.  
- Vegetarian availability: Extremely high; most street stalls are vegetarian‑friendly, though some “pav bhaji” may contain butter made from animal fat—ask for “veg‑butter” if needed.

## Bandra (West) & Khar

**Key places:** Bandra Fort, Mount Mary Church, Bandstand Promenade, Street‑art lanes, Linking Road market

**Mount Mary Church:**  
- Entry: ₹0 (free)  
- Suitability: Gentle slope with handrails; stroller‑compatible, suitable for elderly with limited mobility.  
- Best time of day: 16:00‑18:00 hrs for the sunset view over the Arabian Sea.  
- Seasonal closure: None; interior may be closed on certain feast days—check local notice.

**Trust issue or pricing trap for this area:**  
- Scam: Street vendors selling “authentic” seafood platters often add hidden service charges (₹50‑₹100) without disclosure.  
- Recommended approach: Order at established eateries where the menu lists exact prices, or confirm total cost before ordering.

**Bandra food:**  
- Signature dishes: Chicken Frankie, Parsi dhansak, kebabs, cheese‑filled naan, Bombay‑style biryani.  
- For toddlers and young kids: Soft plain naan, boiled potatoes with a pinch of salt, plain rice with dal, curd (dahi).  
- Vegetarian availability: Good; many stalls serve vegetarian Frankie, paneer tikka, and vegetable biryani, though some Parsi dishes are meat‑based.

## Navi Mumbai (Uran, Kharghar)

**Key places:** Central Park (Kharghar), Pandavkada Falls, Kharghar Hills, Palm Beach Road, Belapur Fort

**Central Park (Kharghar):**  
- Entry: ₹20 adult, ₹10 child (under 12)  
- Suitability: Well‑maintained paved loops, wheelchair‑friendly ramps; toddlers can use strollers, but a few gentle slopes require supervision for elderly with limited balance.  
- Best time of day: 18:00‑20:00 hrs for illuminated walkways and cooler air.  
- Seasonal closure: Monsoon (June‑Sept) may cause temporary water‑logging; park gates close during heavy rain.

**Trust issue or pricing trap for this area:**  
- Scam: Unlicensed “shortcut” guides near Pandavkada Falls charge ₹500‑₹800 for a “guided trek” that is simply a marked trail.  
- Recommended approach: Follow the official signage, use the free map at the park entrance, and avoid paying any guide unless you need a certified trekker for a longer hike.

**Navi Mumbai food:**  
- Signature dishes: Fish thali (rice, varan, fried fish), bhakri with amti, aamras (mango puree), coconut‑laden seafood curry.  
- For toddlers and young kids: Plain boiled rice, dal (lentil soup), curd, soft mashed potatoes.  
- Vegetarian availability: Strong; bhakri, amti, and aamras are vegetarian, though fish thali is non‑veg. Vegetarian stalls are common along Palm Beach Road.

## General Mumbai Notes

**Best time to visit:**  
- **October – February:** Pleasant 20‑28 °C, low humidity; all attractions fully open; Ganesh Chaturthi festivals add cultural vibrancy.  
- **March – May:** Hot 30‑35 °C, humidity rises; beaches crowded, early mornings (06:00‑09:00) recommended for outdoor sites.  
- **June – September:** Monsoon with heavy showers; frequent flooding, ferry to Elephanta often cancelled; indoor museums (Prince of Wales Museum, Dr. Bhau Dhabade Museum) are preferable.

**Transport pricing:**  
- **Taxi/cab:** Metered fare starts ₹22 for the first kilometre, then ₹12 per km; night surcharge 20 % after 22:00 hrs.  
- **Auto‑rickshaw:** Meter rarely used; typical negotiated rate ₹30 per km; shared auto‑rickshaws on Western Line cost ₹10‑₹15 per short hop.  
- **App‑based options:** Uber/Ola standard rates mirror taxi meters; surge pricing may add 1.5‑2× during peak traffic or festivals.

**Dietary notes:**  
- Dominant cuisine: Maharashtrian with liberal use of coconut oil, peanuts, and sesame; seafood is common on the coast. Pork appears mainly in Parsi dishes, not in mainstream street food.  
- Vegetarian options: Abundant across the city; however, many “veg” dishes may contain hidden animal‑derived butter or ghee—ask for “veg‑butter” if strict vegetarian.  
- Allergy risks: Peanuts and sesame seeds are frequent in snacks (bhel, sev); coconut oil is pervasive, which may affect coconut‑allergic travellers.

**Medical:**  
- Nearest high‑quality hospitals: H. J. Mahatma Gandhi Hospital (Byculla) and Kokilaben Dhirubhai Ambani Hospital (Vile Parle). Both have 24‑hour emergency departments and English‑speaking staff.  
- What to carry: Basic first‑aid kit, oral rehydration salts (especially for toddlers during monsoon), any prescribed medication, a copy of vaccination records, and a small stock of over‑the‑counter pain relievers and antihistamines.
