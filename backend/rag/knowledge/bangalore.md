# Bangalore Travel Knowledge

## Routing

**Standard routing: Bangalore → Mysore → Coorg**

Starting in Bangalore (the tech hub) and moving southward to Mysore before heading west to Coorg follows the natural flow of the NH 275 and NH 75 corridors. This order avoids back‑tracking, reduces fatigue (shorter daily drives), and aligns with typical tourist itineraries that combine city culture, heritage, and hill‑station nature.

Driving times:  
- Bangalore → Mysore: 3.5 hours (NH 275, via Srirangapatna)  
- Mysore → Coorg (Madikeri): 2.0 hours (NH 75, via Kushalnagar)

**Alternative routing:** Bangalore → Nandi Hills → Hampi (via NH 44 & NH 50) for a loop that adds a sunrise hill‑top experience and a UNESCO World Heritage day‑trip, extending the drive to about 6 hours total.

## Central Bangalore

**Key places:** Cubbon Park, Bangalore Palace, Vidhana Soudha, Tipu Sultan’s Summer Palace, Bangalore Fort, St. Mark’s Cathedral, UB City, Commercial Street, National Gallery of Modern Art, Bull Temple

**Cubbon Park:**  
- Entry: Free (₹0)  
- Suitability: Flat paved paths, 2 km of shaded walkways – **ideal for toddlers and elderly (wheelchair‑friendly).**  
- Best time of day: Early morning (6 – 8 am) for cool air and fewer crowds.  
- Seasonal closure: None, but heavy monsoon (July‑September) can make trails slippery.

**Bangalore Palace:**  
- Entry: ₹230 adult, ₹115 child (3‑12 yr); toddlers under 3 free.  
- Suitability: 1 km of indoor exhibits and a 500 m garden walk – **suitable for kids 5+; toddlers may find the steep stairs challenging.**  
- Best time: Late afternoon (4 – 6 pm) when light filters through stained glass.  
- Seasonal closure: Closed on major Hindu festivals (e.g., Diwali) for private events.

**Trust issue – “Ticket Over‑charging at Palace”:** Unofficial vendors near the gate sometimes sell “VIP” passes at ₹500 + .  
- Recommended approach: Purchase tickets only at the official counter or via the Bangalore Palace website; carry exact change to avoid inflated “tourist” offers.

**Central Bangalore food:**  
- Signature dishes: Mysore Masala Dosa, Ragi Mudde, Bisi Bele Bath.  
- For toddlers and young kids: Plain Idli, soft Rava Upma, butter‑spread Paratha.  
- Vegetarian availability: Excellent; most stalls are pure vegetarian, with clear labeling for egg or meat dishes.

## South Bangalore

**Key places:** Lalbagh Botanical Garden, Bannerghatta Biological Park, ISKCON Temple, Jayanagar 4th Block, J.P. Road, Gavi Gangadhareshwara Temple, Nandi Hills (though technically north‑west, often accessed from south)

**Lalbagh Botanical Garden:**  
- Entry: ₹20 adult, ₹10 child (3‑12 yr); toddlers free.  
- Suitability: 2 km of well‑maintained walkways, a glasshouse and a lake – **good for toddlers (stroller‑friendly) and seniors (benches every 200 m).**  
- Best time: Early morning (6 – 9 am) to catch the flower show season.  
- Seasonal closure: None; the garden is open year‑round, but the glasshouse may close during heavy rain.

**Bannerghatta Biological Park (Safari & Zoo):**  
- Entry: ₹210 adult, ₹115 child (3‑12 yr); toddlers free for the zoo but not the safari.  
- Suitability: Safari vehicle (4 hrs) – **not suitable for toddlers under 4 (no child seats) or elderly with mobility issues.** The zoo walk is flat and stroller‑friendly.  
- Best time: Mid‑day (11 am‑2 pm) to avoid animal feeding crowds.  
- Seasonal closure: Safari closed during monsoon peaks (July‑early August) for safety.

**Trust issue – “Safari Seat Scam”:** Unauthorized operators claim to offer “private safari” at inflated rates (₹1,200 +).  
- Recommended approach: Book only through the official Bannerghatta website or the ticket counter; verify the vehicle’s registration plate.

**South Bangalore food:**  
- Signature dishes: Ragi Rotti, Akki Rotti, Mysore Pak.  
- For toddlers and young kids: Soft Masala Idli, plain Dosa with a dab of butter, boiled carrots with a pinch of salt.  
- Vegetarian availability: Predominantly vegetarian; many South Indian eateries are pure veg, but confirm as some places serve chicken biryani.

## North Bangalore & Outskirts

**Key places:** Nandi Hills, Turahalli Forest, Hennur Bamboo Forest, Hebbal Lake, ISKCON Bangalore (also central), Sankey Tank, Ulsoor Lake, KR Market, Bangalore International Airport (for logistics)

**Nandi Hills:**  
- Entry: ₹20 adult, ₹10 child (3‑12 yr); toddlers free.  
- Suitability: Steep climb (≈ 3 km) with uneven steps – **not suitable for toddlers or seniors with limited mobility.**  
- Best time: Sunrise (5 – 6 am) for panoramic view and cooler temps.  
- Seasonal closure: Closed during heavy monsoon (July‑September) for landslide risk.

**Turahalli Forest:**  
- Entry: Free (₹0)  
- Suitability: Rough trails, 4 km loop – **suitable for fit families and solo backpackers; not for toddlers or elderly due to uneven terrain and occasional wildlife.**  
- Best time: Early morning (6 – 9 am) for birdwatching and cooler air.  
- Seasonal closure: None, but trail can be slippery after rain.

**Trust issue – “Forest Entry Fee Scam”:** Some “guide” groups demand ₹200 per head at the gate.  
- Recommended approach: No entry fee is required; if approached, politely decline and continue; use a reputable guide service if you need a guide.

**North Bangalore food:**  
- Signature dishes: Ragi Mojjiga, Bisi Bele Bath, Mysore Pak.  
- For toddlers and young kids: Soft Rava Ladoo (sweet), plain Rice with Dal, boiled pumpkin mash.  
- Vegetarian availability: Very high; most stalls are vegetarian, with clear veg/non‑veg signs.

## General Bangalore Notes

**Best time to visit:**  
- **October – February:** Pleasant 15‑25 °C climate; all attractions open; festivals like Karaga (Nov) add cultural vibrancy.  
- **March – May:** Warm (30‑35 °C); early mornings are comfortable; water‑related spots (Hebbal Lake) are pleasant.  
- **June – September:** Monsoon; heavy rain can cause traffic snarls and temporary closures at Nandi Hills and some garden glasshouses; indoor museums remain fully accessible.

**Transport pricing:**  
- **Taxi/cab:** Metered rates start at ₹15 for the first km, then ₹12 per km; airport‑to‑city trips average ₹900‑₹1,200. Negotiate only for flat‑rate out‑station trips.  
- **Auto‑rickshaw:** ₹25 base fare; ₹12 per km thereafter; always request the meter or agree on a price before boarding.  
- **App‑based options:** Uber, Ola, and Rapido (bike) operate city‑wide; surge pricing peaks during evening rush (6 – 9 pm) and festival days.

**Dietary notes:**  
- Dominant cuisine: South Indian (rice, lentils, coconut, tamarind).  
- Allergy risks: Coconut oil is ubiquitous; peanuts appear in many snacks.  
- Vegetarian options: Excellent; most restaurants are pure veg, with a separate “egg” menu where applicable.

**Medical:**  
- Nearest quality hospital: **Fortis Hospital, Bannerghatta Road** (≈ 10 km from central Bangalore). 24‑hour emergency, English‑speaking staff.  
- What to carry: Basic first‑aid kit, any prescription meds, a copy of health insurance, and a small bottle of oral rehydration salts.

## Getting There

**By train:**  
- Nearest railway station: **Bangalore City Railway Station (SBC)**, 4 km from the city centre.  
- Key trains: Karnataka Express (12627, Delhi → Bangalore), Rajdhani Express (12463, Mumbai → Bangalore). Both arrive daily; fare for AC 2‑Tier ranges ₹2,200‑₹2,800.

**By air:**  
- Nearest airport: **Kempegowda International Airport (BLR)**, 40 km north of the city.  
- Airlines: IndiGo, Air India, Vistara, SpiceJet, and international carriers (Emirates, Qatar).  
- Approx. one‑way fare from major Indian metros: ₹3,500‑₹7,500; from Europe/US: ₹45,000‑₹70,000.

**By road:**  
- Key highway: **NH 44** (north‑south corridor) and **NH 75** (east‑west).  
- Nearest state bus depot: **Kempegowda Bus Station (Majestic)**, 2 km from city centre.  
- Inter‑city bus frequency: Every 15 minutes to Mysore, Hubli, and Chennai; typical fare to Mysore ₹300‑₹500.

## Hidden Gems

**Turahalli Forest:**  
- Why special: Offers a quiet trail for mountain‑biking and sunrise birdwatching, rarely crowded.  
- Entry/cost: Free. Reach via a 10‑minute drive from Jalahalli on Turahalli Road; best visited 6 – 9 am.  
- Suitability: Not for toddlers or seniors due to uneven terrain; fit families and backpackers will enjoy.

**Hennur Bamboo Forest:**  
- Why special: A lesser‑known bamboo grove with walking paths, perfect for a serene stroll away from city noise.  
- Entry/cost: Free. Accessible via Hennur Main Road; best in early evening (5 – 7 pm) when light filters through bamboo.  
- Suitability: Flat paths – **suitable for toddlers (stroller‑friendly) and elderly (benches available).**

**Ulsoor Lake (Lake Ulsoor):**  
- Why special: A calm lake with paddle‑boat rentals and a historic island temple, often missed by typical tours.  
- Entry/cost: Free; paddle‑boat ₹150 per hour. Reach via MG Road; sunrise (5 – 6 am) offers misty reflections.  
- Suitability: Gentle boat rides are safe for toddlers (with adult supervision) and seniors.

## Practical Tips

**Connectivity:**  
- Jio, Airtel, and Vi provide strong 4G coverage across Bangalore; dead zones may appear in dense forest areas (Turahalli, Nandi Hills) – carry a portable charger.

**ATMs:**  
- Reliable ATMs at **State Bank of India (SBI) – MG Road**, **HDFC – Koramangala**, and **ICICI – Whitefield**. Cash is preferred in small markets; most restaurants accept cards.

**SIM cards:**  
- Purchase at **Kempegowda International Airport** (Jio, Airtel, Vi kiosks) or at any major mobile shop in city centre. Jio offers the best overall coverage in Bangalore and surrounding outskirts.
