# Delhi / New Delhi Travel Knowledge

## Routing

**Standard routing: Delhi → Agra → Jaipur**

Starting in Delhi allows you to acclimatize to traffic patterns and secure reliable transport before heading south. The Yamuna Expressway to Agra is a straight, high‑speed corridor with rest stops, minimizing fatigue. From Agra, the NH21 to Jaipur traverses relatively flat terrain, letting you finish the classic Golden Triangle without back‑tracking.

Driving times:
- Delhi → Agra: ~3.5 hours (Yamuna Expressway, NH44)
- Agra → Jaipur: ~4.5 hours (NH21 / State Highway 2)

**Alternative routing:** Delhi → Jaipur → Agra (use NH48 to Jaipur, then NH21 back to Agra) – useful if you prefer to explore Jaipur’s markets first or catch an early morning flight out of Jaipur.

## Old Delhi (Shahjahanabad)

**Key places:** Red Fort, Jama Masjid, Chandni Chowk, Paranthe Wali Gali, Gurudwara Sis Ganj

**Red Fort:**
- Entry: ₹35/adult, ₹20/child (3–12 yrs); toddlers under 3 free
- Suitability: 1.2 km of paved pathways, but several steep steps to the museum; **not suitable for toddlers** who cannot manage steps, **moderately suitable for elderly** who can use a wheelchair on the ground floor only.
- Best time: Early morning (08:00–10:00) to avoid heat and crowds.
- Seasonal closure: Closed on Fridays for prayer; open all other days.

**Trust issue or pricing trap for this area:**
- Unauthorized “guide” vendors near the main gate often charge ₹500–₹800 for a “full tour” and may mislead about entry fees.
- Recommended approach: Purchase tickets at the official counter, use the audio guide (₹150) or hire a licensed guide through the ASI website; politely decline street‑side offers.

**Old Delhi food:**
- Signature dishes: Butter chicken, kebabs, chole bhature, jalebi, kachori.
- For toddlers and young kids: Soft idli, plain boiled rice with dal, khichdi, and plain paratha (no spicy chutney).
- Vegetarian availability: Excellent; most stalls serve vegetarian versions of kebabs (paneer tikka) and all chole‑based dishes are veg.

## New Delhi (Lutyens' Delhi)

**Key places:** India Gate, Rashtrapati Bhavan, Parliament House, National Museum, Connaught Place

**India Gate:**
- Entry: Free (₹0)
- Suitability: Wide, level lawns (≈2 km) – **fully suitable for toddlers (stroller‑friendly) and elderly**; benches every 200 m.
- Best time: Sunset (17:30–19:00) for illuminated view.
- Seasonal closure: None; open year‑round.

**Trust issue or pricing trap for this area:**
- Some “photography” vendors near the gate demand ₹300–₹500 for a single click and may overcharge for prints.
- Recommended approach: Use your own smartphone; if you need prints, go to a reputable photo shop in Connaught Place with transparent pricing.

**New Delhi food:**
- Signature dishes: Rogan josh (in upscale cafés), paneer tikka, aloo tikki, street‑style chaat.
- For toddlers and young kids: Soft poha, plain curd rice, boiled potatoes with a pinch of salt, and mild dal‑paratha.
- Vegetarian availability: Very high; most cafés have a dedicated veg menu; however, some “special” dishes contain ghee or butter, so ask for “no ghee” if needed.

## South Delhi (Hauz Khas & Mehrauli)

**Key places:** Hauz Khas Complex, Qutub Minar, Mehrauli Archaeological Park, Garden of Five Senses

**Qutub Minar:**
- Entry: ₹30/adult, ₹20/child (3–12 yrs); toddlers under 3 free
- Suitability: 1.5 km of paved walk with uneven stones; **not suitable for toddlers** who cannot navigate loose gravel, **caution for elderly** due to steep stairs to the top (skip the climb, enjoy the base).
- Best time: Early morning (07:00–09:00) before the sun heats the stone.
- Seasonal closure: Closed on Fridays for prayer; also closed during heavy monsoon (July‑August) for safety.

**Trust issue or pricing trap for this area:**
- “Fast‑track” ticket sellers near the gate often claim they can bypass the queue for an extra ₹200–₹400, which is illegal.
- Recommended approach: Use the official e‑ticket portal (https://www.tourism.gov.in) and arrive at least 30 minutes before opening; no need for any “fast‑track” service.

**South Delhi food:**
- Signature dishes: Mughlai biryani, kebab rolls, dal makhani, thandai.
- For toddlers and young kids: Soft upma, plain boiled rice with ghee‑free dal, mild paneer cubes, and banana‑flavored lassi.
- Vegetarian availability: Strong; most restaurants serve a full veg menu; watch for hidden meat stock in broth‑based soups.

## General Delhi Notes

**Best time to visit:**
- **October – March:** Pleasant 8‑20 °C, all monuments fully open, festivals (Diwali, Holi) increase crowd but add cultural vibrancy.
- **April – June:** Hot 30‑45 °C; early morning visits recommended; water‑intensive attractions (e.g., gardens) may have reduced watering.
- **July – September:** Monsoon; heavy rain can cause temporary closures at Qutub Minar and Mehrauli Park; indoor museums remain accessible; travel may be delayed on flooded roads.

**Transport pricing:**
- **Taxi/cab:** Metered fares from Indira Gandhi International Airport to central Delhi start at ₹500; city‑wide base fare ₹50, then ₹12 per km; negotiate only for flat‑rate packages (e.g., “tourist package”) and verify the total before boarding.
- **Auto/rickshaw:** Flag‑down rate ₹30 for the first km, then ₹12 per km; always ask for the meter or agree on a price (≈₹150 for a 5 km ride) before starting.
- **App‑based options:** Ola and Uber operate city‑wide; typical city ride ₹120‑₹250; use the “share‑ride” option for cheaper fares; surge pricing spikes during peak festival evenings.

**Dietary notes:**
- Dominant cuisine: North Indian with heavy use of ghee, butter, and cream; common allergens include dairy, nuts (especially cashew), and coconut oil in some street‑side sweets.
- Vegetarian options: Abundant across street stalls and restaurants; however, verify “pure veg” status as many “veg” dishes may contain butter or paneer cooked in ghee.

**Medical:**
- Nearest quality hospital: All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS), Delhi – located in South Delhi, 5 km from Connaught Place; 24‑hour emergency, English‑speaking staff.
- What to carry: Basic first‑aid kit (band‑aids, antiseptic wipes, oral rehydration salts), any prescribed medication, a copy of health insurance, and a portable water‑purification bottle (to avoid stomach upset from tap water).
