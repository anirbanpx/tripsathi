# Haridwar Travel Knowledge

## Routing

**Standard routing: Delhi → Haridwar → Rishikesh**

The circuit starts from Delhi because NH 34 (Delhi‑Meerut) merges into NH 58 at Ghaziabad, which is the most direct, well‑maintained highway to Haridwar. Arriving in Haridwar first lets travellers acclimatise to the river‑front climate and complete the mandatory Ganga Aarti before heading north to Rishikesh, where the terrain becomes hillier and the roads narrower. This order minimises fatigue and avoids back‑tracking on the congested NH 58.

Driving times:  
- Delhi → Haridwar: 5–6 hours (NH 58, 240 km)  
- Haridwar → Rishikesh: 1 hour (NH 58, 20 km)

**Alternative routing:** Arrive via Dehradun (NH 7) and descend the Haridwar‑Dehradun stretch (NH 334) to Haridwar; this is useful when Delhi traffic is heavy or when the traveller is already in the hill stations of Uttarakhand.

## Haridwar City Core

**Key places:** Har Ki Pauri, Mansa Devi Temple, Chandi Devi Temple, Daksheswar Mahadev, Ganga Aarti, Sapt Rishi Ashram

**Har Ki Pauri:**  
- Entry: Free (donations optional)  
- Suitability: Flat, well‑paved promenade; wheelchair‑friendly ramps; **suitable for toddlers (stroller‑accessible) and elderly**.  
- Best time of day: Early morning (5:30–7:00 am) for sunrise; evening (7:00–8:30 pm) for Ganga Aarti.  
- Seasonal closure: None, but monsoon (July‑August) can cause temporary water‑level rise; stay on marked pathways.

**Mansa Devi Temple (Haridwar):**  
- Entry: ₹5 adult, ₹2 child (under 12) – no entry fee for toddlers under 3.  
- Suitability: 300 m steep climb of stone steps; **not suitable for toddlers or mobility‑limited elderly**. Carry a carrier for children under 5.  
- Best time of day: Mid‑morning (9:00–11:00 am) when crowds thin.  
- Seasonal closure: Closed on Tuesdays (temple’s “Mool Tithi”).

**Chandi Devi Temple (Haridwar):**  
- Entry: ₹10 adult, ₹5 child (3–12) – free for toddlers.  
- Suitability: Cable‑car (rope‑way) from the base; **suitable for toddlers (cable‑car allowed) and elderly**; however, the upper platform has uneven stone.  
- Best time of day: Late afternoon (4:00–5:30 pm) for panoramic sunset view.  
- Seasonal closure: Rope‑way closed during heavy monsoon (mid‑July to early September).

**Daksheswar Mahadev:**  
- Entry: ₹5 adult, ₹2 child – free for toddlers.  
- Suitability: Small shrine on a low hill; **suitable for toddlers and elderly**; no steep steps.  
- Best time of day: Early afternoon (12:30–2:00 pm) when the sun illuminates the shiva lingam.  
- Seasonal closure: None.

**Ganga Aarti (Har Ki Pauri):**  
- Entry: Free (donations welcome).  
- Suitability: Large open area; **suitable for toddlers (stroller‑friendly) and elderly**; keep a close watch as crowds surge.  
- Best time of day: Sunset (around 7:00 pm, varies seasonally).  
- Seasonal closure: None, but monsoon water rise may limit visibility.

**Sapt Rishi Ashram:**  
- Entry: ₹5 adult, ₹2 child – free for toddlers.  
- Suitability: Quiet riverside garden; **suitable for toddlers and elderly**; gentle walking paths.  
- Best time of day: Early morning (6:00–8:00 am) for calm river sounds.  
- Seasonal closure: None.

**Temple‑donation scam (Har Ki Pauri area):**  
- Some “volunteers” approach tourists offering to arrange a “special aarti” for a fee of ₹500–₹1,000, which is a fabricated service.  
- Recommended approach: Insist on the official free aarti; if you wish to donate, use the official donation box near the aarti platform.

**Haridwar food:**  
- Signature dishes: Kachori with aloo stuffing, Aloo Puri, Kesar Halwa, Gajar ka halwa, Lassi (sweet & salted), Chaat with sev.  
- For toddlers and young kids: Plain boiled rice with dal, soft idli, plain khichdi, boiled potatoes, fresh fruit slices (banana, papaya).  
- Vegetarian availability: Almost all street stalls are vegetarian; meat is rare and usually limited to non‑vegetarian “Mughlai” stalls on the outskirts.

## Rajaji National Park Fringe (Haridwar Outskirts)

**Key places:** Rajaji National Park (East Gate), Corbett‑style Jungle Safari, Elephant‑back rides, Ganga‑River Birdwatching Point

**Rajaji National Park – East Gate:**  
- Entry: ₹200 adult, ₹100 child (3–12); toddlers under 3 free but not allowed on safari.  
- Suitability: Safari entry point has paved parking; **not suitable for toddlers (no safari) and limited for elderly due to long jeep rides (up to 4 hours) and uneven terrain inside the park**.  
- Best time of day: Early morning (6:00–9:00 am) for wildlife activity; also late afternoon (4:00–6:00 pm).  
- Seasonal closure: Park closed during heavy monsoon (mid‑July to early September) due to flooding.

**Jungle Safari (Jeep):**  
- Entry: Included in park fee; additional ₹150 per hour for private jeep.  
- Suitability: Rough gravel tracks; **not suitable for toddlers; elderly travellers should limit ride to ≤2 hours and request a cushioned seat**.  
- Best time of day: Same as park entry.  
- Seasonal closure: Same as park.

**Elephant‑back rides (Haridwar side):**  
- Entry: ₹500 per person (adult), ₹300 child (3–12).  
- Suitability: Short 30‑minute ride on a gentle path; **suitable for toddlers (must be seated on adult’s lap) and elderly (slow pace), but not recommended for those with back problems**.  
- Best time of day: Mid‑morning (10:00–11:30 am) when elephants are rested.  
- Seasonal closure: Closed during monsoon and extreme heat (>38 °C).

**Birdwatching Point (Ganga River bank):**  
- Entry: Free.  
- Suitability: Flat, shaded benches; **suitable for toddlers (stroller‑friendly) and elderly**.  
- Best time of day: Dawn (5:30–7:00 am) for migratory birds.  
- Seasonal closure: None, but water‑level rise in monsoon may limit access.

**Safari over‑charging trap:**  
- Unlicensed operators often quote “₹2,000 per jeep” for a “private” safari but the vehicle is shared with other tourists, inflating the price.  
- Recommended approach: Book the safari through the official Uttarakhand Forest Department counter (online or at the park gate) and verify the vehicle registration number before boarding.

**Rajaji fringe food:**  
- Local dishes: Kumaoni Ras Bhaat (rice with lentil broth), Bhatt ki Churi (black soybean cake), Gahat ki Dal, fresh river fish (for non‑vegetarians).  
- For toddlers and young kids: Soft boiled rice, plain dal, mashed sweet potatoes, fresh curd (dahi).  
- Vegetarian availability: High; most stalls serve only vegetarian Kumaoni fare; fish is limited to designated “non‑veg” kiosks.

## General Haridwar Notes

**Best time to visit:**  
- **October – March:** Cool, dry weather (10‑20 °C); all temples and the park are fully open; evenings are ideal for Ganga Aarti.  
- **April – June:** Warm (25‑35 °C); early mornings are pleasant; heat can be intense for toddlers; plan indoor activities during midday.  
- **July – September:** Monsoon; heavy rain, river rise, and Rajaji Park closure; some temple steps become slippery—extra caution required.  
- **November (full moon):** Special “Maha Aarti” draws large crowds; book transport early.

**Transport pricing:**  
- **Taxi/cab:** Metered fares from Delhi airport to Haridwar start at ₹2,200; within city, metered rates are ₹12 per km; negotiate only for out‑station trips without a meter (typical fare ₹1,500 for 20 km).  
- **Auto‑rickshaw:** Fixed rates for short hops (e.g., Har Ki Pauri to Mansa Devi) are ₹30–₹50; longer trips (city to Rajaji gate) are ₹150–₹200.  
- **App‑based options:** Uber/Ola operate in Haridwar; base fare ₹80 plus ₹10 per km; auto‑rickshaw option available via “Uber Auto” with similar pricing.

**Dietary notes:**  
- Dominant cuisine: North‑Indian with Kumaoni influences; heavy use of ghee, mustard oil, and fresh herbs.  
- Allergy risks: Coconut oil is common in sweets; dairy (curd, paneer) is prevalent—carry alternatives if lactose intolerant.  
- Vegetarian options: Abundant; most street food is vegetarian; only a few “non‑veg” stalls serve fish or chicken, clearly marked.

**Medical:**  
- Nearest quality hospital: **Maa Tara Devi Hospital, Haridwar** (≈5 km from Har Ki Pauri, 24‑hour emergency, ICU, and a dedicated pediatric ward).  
- Recommended to carry: Basic first‑aid kit, any personal prescription medicines, a copy of vaccination records (especially for children), and a small stock of oral rehydration salts for monsoon‑related stomach upset.
